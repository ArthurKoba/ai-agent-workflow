## Diagram construction — graph first, geometry second

1. List every symbol and directed relation before drawing: node IDs, inputs, outputs, decision alternatives, failure paths and return loops. Use the applicable diagram-symbol standard (e.g. ISO 5807; regional standards only when required): rectangle for process, diamond for decision, parallelogram for input/output. A component-level functional diagram does not substitute for an executable decision algorithm.
2. Arrange nodes on a regular grid with room for readable labels. Use concise labels in the requested document language. Draw connectors orthogonally, with consistent stroke weight, one clear direction and no line through another block or its text. Put caption, method and long explanations **below the figure**, not inside its geometry.
3. For a straight **primary path between two blocks**, create **one direct edge** from its source contour to its destination contour. Do NOT split that edge through a transparent/invisible intermediate node to attach another branch: diagrams.net autorouting can add a visually disastrous zigzag or disconnect.
4. For a **secondary branch** joining a main trunk, determine direction and junction notation from the selected standard and project contract. Keep the primary path continuous and its connection to the receiving block clear. Use a junction dot at a real merge **when required**; omit it when the selected notation forbids it. Avoid duplicate heads, competing arrows and unnecessary elbows immediately before the receiving block.
5. Place separate side arrivals at separate heights. Crossing two lines without connecting them is not a merge. If routing requires a join placeholder in the editor, it must not become an endpoint or waypoint **on the primary path**. The visual junction must be unambiguous, with the prescribed marker (if any), no unintended gap and no overlap.
6. Label **both** exits of each diamond, e.g. yes/no in an English-language diagram; keep labels next to the correct segment, outside symbols. Route reject and retry paths in free lanes. Never let one branch pass through an unrelated decision or connector label.
7. Attach arrows to the **visible perimeter**, not the shape's bounding rectangle. For parallelograms, compute the sloped side exactly. If the algorithm returns from an output parallelogram to an input parallelogram, normally leave from the **bottom midpoint of the output** and enter the **top midpoint of the input**; route along a separate outer corridor. Do not attach to the sloping sides when the figure requires top/bottom.
8. On every change, inspect **all** connections and decisions. Moving one block can make previously accepted connections, labels or returns invalid. Preserve the meaning and unaffected verified elements.
