# Scientific Writing — Manuscripts and Editorial Consistency

Use to draft, edit or adapt scientific articles and research reports.
This skill governs composition, scientific prose and venue formatting.
Evidence retrieval, mathematical validation and independent scientific
review belong to their corresponding skills.

## Plan the argument

Identify the research question, audience, verified contribution,
available evidence, genre and current journal or institutional
instructions. Choose sections that allow the reader to trace:
problem and relevant prior work → objective → method or model →
implementation/experiment where applicable → results → discussion,
limitations and conclusion. IMRaD is a useful content audit, not a
mandatory heading scheme. Do not force an article into a dissertation
or conference-abstract structure or prescribe universal page counts.

Keep distinct functions distinct:
- **Introduction:** establish the gap, significance and measurable aim,
  without presenting unverified results as motivation.
- **Background/theory:** cite established methods instead of repeating
  textbook material; explain the assumptions needed for this study.
- **Method/design:** describe the author's specific contribution,
  inputs, transformations, outputs, parameters and limitations.
- **Experiment/results:** distinguish experimental setup and actual
  observations; retain essential controls, metrics and conditions.
- **Discussion/conclusion:** interpret evidence against alternatives,
  disclose shortcomings and relate the result to the initial aim.
Sections may be combined when the venue or discipline calls for it.

## Write precise scientific prose

Give each paragraph a discernible claim and supporting reasoning.
Define terms and abbreviations at first use, then preserve their
meaning across prose, tables and equations. Explain why a method
was chosen before describing how it works; avoid irrelevant theory
and repeated restatements of the same numerical results.
Distinguish observed facts, cited claims, calculations, estimates,
assumptions and hypotheses. Avoid unsupported causality,
exaggerated novelty and stronger conclusions than the data allow.
Use the document language and the target venue's voice, grammar
and typography; neither third-person voice nor a fixed sentence
length is universal. When editing an author's established voice,
infer it from verified final texts, not draft fragments.

## Tables, figures and references

Use a table for precise values and multivariate comparison; use a
plot for trends and distributions. Name quantities, units,
normalization and uncertainty where material. Refer to every table
and figure in text and explain the finding rather than reciting
individual cells. Keep captions self-contained enough to identify
the object, conditions and data origin. Preserve stable references
to sources, figures and formulas; use the active publishing
toolchain's syntax, not a project-specific hardcoded convention.
Scientific correctness of their content requires separate checks.

## Edit and publish from one source of truth

Before submission, verify current venue rules for structure,
author metadata, references, disclosures, figures, accessibility
and acceptable files. Keep one authoritative editable manuscript,
then regenerate and visually inspect derived DOCX/PDF output.
Recheck terminology, section continuity, cross-references,
numbers and abstract/conclusion after substantive edits.
Record submitted, accepted and published versions separately;
an export or submission does not prove editorial acceptance.

**Complete when:** the draft makes a coherent evidence-bounded
argument, follows the selected venue, and renders faithfully
from its canonical editable source.
