# Scientific Novelty and Contribution Audit

Use when identifying a scientific gap, assessing novelty, defining
defensible research contributions, reviewing novelty claims, or preparing
substantive results for a paper or thesis. This is **verification of
claims**, not automatic novelty certification.

## Identify the closest prior work

1. State the proposed result as a falsifiable proposition: **what is
   new; relative to which alternatives; under what conditions; in
   which measurable or theoretical sense**.
2. Search the relevant research literature and trace the closest
   prior art, including older foundational work when needed. Compare
   actual methods, assumptions and demonstrated outcomes, not just
   article titles. Record search coverage, sources, date and access limits.
3. Build a small comparative matrix: source/version, comparable task,
   inputs and constraints, common method, distinguishing operation,
   metric/proof, limitations and what remains unknown. If the full
   source is inaccessible, never infer that it lacks a feature from
   a silent abstract or missing keyword.
4. Identify where a combination of known components might form a
   legitimate contribution: formally defined interaction, measurable
   effect, new criterion, mechanism, model, proof or valid application
   to a new class of problems. The combination must itself be
   understood and evidenced.

## Do not confuse categories of achievement

- **Novel scientific result:** substantiated contribution compared to
  relevant prior work, with a reason and scope.
- **Engineering implementation:** code, system architecture or device
  implementing some method; its existence alone is not new science.
- **Practical usefulness / implementation in practice:** observed
  utility or documented use; not by itself proof of scientific novelty.
- **Publication or conference talk:** dissemination, not proof of the
  claim's correctness or priority.
- **Personal contribution:** the parts demonstrably created or derived
  by the author, separately from known techniques and others' work.

"No analogues found", "first", "proved", "guarantees", "improves" and
"new method" require evidence commensurate with that wording.
A limited search supports only "not identified in the searched
scope", not universal absence. A promising hypothesis is not
a completed result; do not use publication count as a novelty score.

## Evidence gate for a contribution

For every proposed claim answer:
1. Exactly what is asserted and what is already known?
2. Which source proves the nearest comparable baseline?
3. What is the distinctive operation/model/criterion, and why could
   it change the assessed property?
4. Which proof, calculation or controlled experiment establishes
   the distinction, and what were the input conditions and metrics?
5. Are there negative cases, trade-offs, untested populations or
   contradictory sources? Is the result specific to one configuration?
6. What is the author's **documented** intellectual contribution,
   and how does the claim trace to the manuscript and evidence?

Keep a bounded claim-evidence matrix and revise or withdraw claims
when later results contradict them. A degree/dissertation's
"defendable proposition" should be a specific substantive result
and applicability boundary, not a chapter title or future plan.
Never manufacture personal credit, experiments, originality
or advisor agreement.

**Completion:** claims are supported, narrowed to their actual
conditions, or explicitly marked as hypotheses needing further work.
