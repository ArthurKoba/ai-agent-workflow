# Scientific Writing and Editorial Consistency

Use when drafting, condensing, editing or polishing scientific
prose; applying a journal or institutional style profile; or
maintaining a canonical manuscript and derived export. This
Skill governs **argumentation and prose**, not evidence discovery,
math verification or the choice of a particular publishing tool.

## Before drafting

Establish document genre, discipline, intended readers,
authoritative venue requirements, the research question and
verified results. Read the current manuscript and its adjoining
claims instead of rewriting disconnected paragraphs. When
evidence or agreement is missing, mark it as a question; do not
fill it with rhetorical certainty.

## Write a checkable scientific argument

- One paragraph should advance a clear, identifiable idea;
  order material from premise to condition to result and
  interpretation. Define technical terms and abbreviations
  when first used, and then use them consistently.
- Explain **why** a particular operation, model or test is
  necessary before listing what it does. For important methods
  describe the input, transformation, output, assumptions and
  constraints; avoid textbook digressions unrelated to the
  claimed difference.
- Distinguish confirmed evidence, author interpretations,
  assumptions, hypotheses and recommendations. Avoid wording
  stronger than the source or current experiment warrants.
- State the purpose of each numerical comparison and explain
  relevant changes, costs, trade-offs and validity boundaries
  rather than mechanically repeating table cells.
- Prefer concise, precise prose. Third-person/impersonal style
  and local typography may be suitable but the actual venue,
  language and author requirements decide. Never distort a
  mathematical condition merely to shorten a sentence.
- When adapting an author's style, infer it from **verified
  final** texts; do not confuse publisher formatting, isolated
  supervisor edits or an AI-generated draft with a proven
  author's individual voice. Preserve the original when
  restoring a published work, and place proposals separately.

## Sources, equations and figures in prose

Introduce a figure or table in text, give an informative
caption and explain its relation to the claim. Define every
unit, variable and cited item in its proper context. Use the
applicable venue conventions for reference numbering,
symbols and bibliography. Do not invent a universal citation,
font, page or image rule from another research project.

When a project has one authoritative authoring format
(e.g. Markdown with LaTeX), correct source files, not only
the generated DOCX/PDF; re-export and verify the final
artifact. Do not maintain competing editable manuscripts.
Versioned submissions must remain separately identifiable,
and a visual/export check is distinct from factual review.

## Manuscript structure and publication formatting

For research articles and comparable manuscripts, make the research problem,
relevant prior work, objective, contribution, methods, validated results,
limitations and conclusions traceable. Use IMRaD only when suitable for the
discipline and venue; never force a universal chapter structure or word count.
Before submission, verify the current publisher or institution instructions
for metadata, sections, citation style, figures, tables, disclosure statements,
templates and accepted file formats. Record the source and version. Keep
canonical source and exported/submitted versions distinguishable. Check the
rendered final document, not only the source or an export success message.

## Editing and review

After each substantive edit check that the objective,
claim, results, conclusion, terminology, equations, units,
citations, figures and other affected sections still agree.
Condensation is successful only when meaning and all
essential reproducibility conditions survive; move detailed
protocol to a referenced appendix when appropriate.
For an editor's comment, record the location, intended
change, revised position and verified resolution.

**Completion:** the prose accurately describes the current
evidence with consistent terms and is reproducibly rendered
to the required venue format; style polish alone is not
scientific validation.
